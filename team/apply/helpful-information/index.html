Executive Recruitment · Helpful Information

Applying to the exec, demystified.

Applying to become an ALSA executive is a real process, and a deliberately thorough one. This walks you through what each stage involves, so there are no surprises and you can put your best foot forward.

The process is designed to see how you think and how you work, not to catch you out. The steps are the same for everyone, and the effort is deliberate: the wrong person in a role costs the team more than an empty seat, so we ask for a real application and give real consideration in return.

What You'll Submit

The application, by track.

Officers, including the marketing team

A CV, a short video, written questions, and a task relevant to the role, followed by an interview. The video and task are there because officers own a portfolio, so we need to see how you plan and execute, not just what you have done before.

Interns

A CV, written questions, and an interview, with no task or video. The intern tier is about potential, not experience, so the ask is lighter by design.

The 2027 round is aimed at recruiting Officer and Marketing team roles. Interns and cabinet are not part of this intake, but the intern track is described here for reference.

Your CV and Written Answers

The written part.

  • Keep it concrete. One to two pages. Lead with recent, relevant experience, and make it specific: what you did and what changed because of it.
  • Answer the actual question. For the written questions, answer what is asked, give a real example, and keep to any word limit.
  • Show why ALSA, and why this role. "I want to get involved" is not an answer. Tell us what draws you to this portfolio and what you would bring to it.
  • Be honest about where you are. You do not need a CV that already impresses. You need to show you want it and can back that up.
  • Proofread, then have someone else read it. Small errors are avoidable, and avoiding them is part of the signal.
The Video

Your recorded answers.

Officer and marketing applicants record short answers to set questions. It feels unnatural because it is, so a little preparation goes a long way.

  • Set the scene. A quiet space with no interruptions, a tidy background, and light in front of you rather than behind.
  • Look at the camera, not yourself. It reads as eye contact. Sit up, slow down, and treat it like a conversation.
  • Structure your answer. A clear point, a specific example, then the result. One point made well beats three half-made.
  • Do one practice run and watch it back. Uncomfortable, and genuinely the fastest way to improve.
The Task

A preview of the real work.

  • Treat it like the job. The task mirrors what the role actually involves, so approach it the way you would approach real work for the team.
  • Show your thinking, not just the output. How you plan, prioritise, and make decisions matters as much as the final result.
  • Follow the brief and the deadline. Doing what was asked, on time, is the first thing we are looking for. It is the whole role in miniature.
The Interview

Meeting the team.

  • Know your own application. Anything in your CV or answers is fair game. Be ready to talk about it with specifics.
  • Prepare for the classics. Why ALSA, why this role, and a time you led or delivered something. Real examples, not rehearsed lines.
  • Bring questions. Thoughtful questions about the work and the team show genuine interest.
  • Be a person, not a performance. We are deciding whether we want to work with you for a semester. Warmth and honesty go a long way.
If You're Offered a Place

The two-week onboarding.

Every new exec member starts with a two-week onboarding period, paired with an onboarding partner who is your first point of contact and the person you report to. During those two weeks you send brief, regular updates on what you are working on.

At the end, cabinet votes to confirm your place. This is mutual: we assess whether the role is a fit, and you decide whether it is right for you. It is how we stay accountable for who is on the team.

Read the full picture of what being on the exec asks of you in the Executive Standards.

Common Questions

FAQs.

Do I need previous exec or legal experience?
No. Officers need to show they can plan and execute; interns are chosen on potential. Non-legal work, leadership, and involvement all count. Show what you have done and what it taught you.
Which track should I apply for?
Officers own a portfolio and are a natural fit for second and third years ready to lead. Interns support an officer and suit first and second years and international students, no experience needed. The 2027 round is recruiting Officer and Marketing team roles.
Can I apply for more than one role?
Focus your application on the role that fits you best. If you are genuinely open to more than one, say so, and we will consider you where you are strongest.
How much time does a role take?
Officer roles are roughly 5 to 10 hours a week, with more in event weeks. Each role page sets out the specifics.
When do applications open?
Applications for the 2027 executive team open soon. Follow us on our socials to hear the moment they do.
